How CAM Statement Check computes

← Back to the checker

Every number comes from the lease terms and statement lines you enter. Nothing is looked up, estimated or filled in. If a required input is missing, the page stops and lists what it needs instead of assuming zero.

1. Which lines count

Each statement line is marked Recoverable, Lease excludes it or Not sure. Excluded lines never count. Not-sure lines are computed both ways: once left out (the low end) and once included (the high end). The result is a range instead of a guess. Only you can read your lease's exclusion list, so the page never decides a line is excluded on its own.

2. Gross-up (only if your lease has one)

Variable lines are raised to the occupancy your lease names. Fixed lines (taxes, insurance and the like) and the fee line are never grossed up.

factor = named occupancy ÷ actual average occupancy (only when actual < named; never below 1)
variable line, adjusted = amount × factor

This is the usual linear gross-up. Some leases define it differently, for example per-expense or with a different occupancy measure. If yours does, its definition wins. If the statement's amounts are already grossed up, choose that option. The page will then not recompute the gross-up and will ask for the actual amounts instead.

3. Management / administrative fee

If your lease limits the fee, the page computes the allowed fee from the lines you tick as "in fee base", after exclusions and gross-up. It counts the lower of the charged fee and the allowed fee.

fee base = Σ adjusted amounts of counted lines ticked "in fee base"
fee allowed = fee % × fee base
fee counted = min(fee charged, fee allowed)

Leases differ on what the fee is computed on. Taxes, insurance and utilities are often left out of the base. Tick lines to match your lease's wording.

4. Your share

pool = Σ adjusted counted expense lines + fee counted
share = your square feet ÷ the lease's divisor (or the percentage your lease states)

If you enter the share the statement printed, the page compares it with your lease's definition and flags any gap above 0.005 percentage points. A different divisor is one of the most common sources of disagreement, for example one that leaves out vacant space or large tenants.

5. Cap on controllable expenses (net leases)

Your share of the controllable lines (plus the fee, if you tick that it counts) is limited by the cap. The excess comes off your share.

controllable (your share) = share × (Σ adjusted controllable lines [+ fee counted])
year-over-year: limit = last year's allowable amount × (1 + r)
compounding: limit = base amount × (1 + r)n
not compounding: limit = base amount × (1 + r × n)
your share = share before cap − max(0, controllable − limit)

"Cumulative", "non-cumulative" and "compounding" are used inconsistently from lease to lease. That is why the page asks for the formula, not the label. For the year-over-year form, "last year's allowable amount" means the lower of last year's actual controllable charge and last year's cap. Caps are applied only to net leases. With a base-year or expense-stop structure, the page shows the uncapped result and says so.

6. The verdict

The statement's charge is compared with the range (low = Not-sure lines left out, high = included), with a $1 tolerance:

  • Above — higher than the high end. Even counting every uncertain line, the statement exceeds what your lease terms produce.
  • Within — inside the range. Whether it's right depends on the uncertain lines.
  • Matches — equal to your lease terms' result within $1 (no uncertain lines).
  • Below — lower than the low end. Check your entries first.

"Your percentage of a line" figures (for example, what an excluded line adds) are the share times the line's adjusted amount. Under a base-year or expense-stop lease, the real effect can be smaller if the pool sits near the base or the stop.

What this does not do

  • It does not verify that expenses happened or were reasonable. Only invoices and the general ledger can show that, which is why the draft asks for them.
  • It does not read your lease, decide legal questions, compute deadlines, or tell you to pay or dispute.
  • It does not handle amortized capital items, partial-year occupancy, multiple expense pools (for example separate CAM, tax and insurance pools with different shares), or tenant-specific carve-outs. Run each pool separately if your statement splits them.
  • It stores nothing. Download your notes if you want to keep them. When you close the page, everything you typed is gone.
