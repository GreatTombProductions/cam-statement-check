Where the numbers come from

Only from you: your lease's cost provisions and your landlord's reconciliation statement. The page uses no external data and sends nothing anywhere. The formulas are on the methodology page.

Background reading

These practitioner guides describe the review points this page checks. They are background, not authority. Your lease controls.

  • Mohr Partners — "Mastering CAM Audits" (April 2025). An audit verifies "that the shared expenses billed to tenants align with their lease agreements". It covers caps that "typically set a maximum annual percentage increase (e.g., 5%)", gross-up miscalculation and capital improvements as costs tenants should not bear.
  • StrataFolio — "CAM Reconciliation Red Flags That Trigger Tenant Audits" (April 2026). It names "misapplication of cumulative vs. non-cumulative caps" and notes that roof, structural, major mechanical and parking-lot projects in CAM draw recoverability questions.

When arithmetic isn't enough

  • Free small-business advising: America's Small Business Development Centers, SCORE mentors, and the SBA's local assistance finder. They can help you think through the decision and the conversation with your landlord.
  • Lease auditors review the landlord's books against the lease. Some work on contingency, paid from what they recover. Your lease's audit clause may limit who can audit and when.
  • A real-estate attorney, for questions about what a clause means, how notice must be given, or what happens if you withhold payment.
